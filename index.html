How can we help more people maintain cognitive functioning as they age?

Maxwell ElliottAssistant Professor of Psychology, University of Minnesota

Some older adults develop dementia and disability around retirement. Others keep exceptional cognitive function into their 90s. I study these individual differences in cognitive and brain aging.

I’m recruiting PhD students and a postdoc

Go big

I use cohort studies and large datasets to find, across whole populations, who ages well and who doesn’t.

The Dunedin Study

1,037 people born in Dunedin, New Zealand, in 1972–73 and assessed from birth to age 45, including brain MRI at 45. Following one cohort for decades shows who ages fast, who ages slow, and what came before.

Brain-age in midlife is associated with accelerated biological aging and cognitive decline in a longitudinal birth cohortMolecular Psychiatry, 2021

UK Biobank

About half a million UK adults, aged 40 to 69 when they joined in 2006–2010, with brain imaging for a large subset. It lets measures built in one cohort be tested in very large samples.

ADNI

The Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative has followed older adults with and without cognitive impairment since 2004, with repeated brain scans.

Go small

In tandem, I study smaller, informative groups in depth, to improve how we measure aging and to test targeted hypotheses.

One person at a time

Measurement error in a single standard scan can hide real change in one person’s brain.

Cluster scanning

Instead of one long scan, many rapid scans in a single session, combined into a more precise measurement.

Precision brain morphometry using cluster scanningImaging Neuroscience, 2024

One year apart

Measured precisely at both ends, brain aging differs between people even across a single year.

Precision estimates of longitudinal brain aging capture unexpected individual differences in one yearNature Communications, 2026

DunedinPACNI

A single brain image can estimate a person’s longitudinal Pace of Aging, a measure developed in the Dunedin Study.

DunedinPACNI estimates the longitudinal Pace of Aging from a single brain image to track health and diseaseNature Aging, 2025

Find the windows

Prevention, earlier

Throughout my work, I look for critical windows in the lifespan for prevention and treatment. Much of it looks at midlife, decades before dementia usually appears.
